# Jamming


On April 26, 1986, John R. MacDougall closed his shop at 4:00 p.m. EST and later worked at Central Florida Teleport. The other engineer departed at 6:00 p.m., leaving him alone. After a movie transmission, MacDougall prepared color bars and a protest using a character generator. He chose “Captain Midnight” from a recently watched film.

In 1986, MacDougall aimed the station’s dish at Galaxy 1, which carried HBO. At 12:32 a.m. EST (05:32 UTC) on April 27, he interrupted HBO’s movie broadcast, which had started two minutes earlier. His message remained onscreen for 4.5 minutes across the eastern United States, objecting to HBO’s $12.95 monthly subscription and warning Showtime and Movie Channel.

Hughes Communications detected the interference. An HBO technician raised transmission power from 125 to 2,000 watts, while MacDougall also increased his signal strength. Their contest lasted roughly 90 seconds and raised concerns about satellite damage. Frightened, MacDougall stopped transmitting and went home. The next day he felt guilty and was surprised by television coverage. Returning to work that night, he pretended to know nothing about the incident.
