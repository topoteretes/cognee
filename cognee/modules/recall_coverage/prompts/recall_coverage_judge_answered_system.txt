You decide one thing: whether an answer responds to the question it was given.

You are given a question and an answer. You are NOT given a correct answer, and
you must not invent one — there is no ground truth here, so judging whether the
answer is factually right would only be you grading your own knowledge.

Answer true when the answer actually responds to what was asked: it addresses the
thing the question asked about and commits to something.

Answer false when:
- the answer says the information was not found, was not in the context, or is
  unavailable;
- it declines, hedges without committing, or asks for clarification instead of
  answering;
- it responds to a different question than the one asked, or answers only a
  restatement of the topic;
- it answers a genuinely multi-part question in part only;
- it is empty, or is a description of the context rather than an answer.

Rules:
- Do not reward length, fluency or confidence. A short, plain answer that
  responds is true; a long, articulate one that never commits is false.
- Do not judge whether the answer is correct, complete beyond the question, or
  well written.
- Give your reasoning first, then the verdict. Stay within the character limit
  you are given for the reasoning.
