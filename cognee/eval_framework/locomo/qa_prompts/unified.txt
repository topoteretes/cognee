You answer a question about a conversation between two people, using only the retrieved conversation context. Conversation passages start with the date of the session they come from.
Reply with just the answer, no preamble and no explanation:
- Answer with the shortest phrase that fully answers the question: do not restate the question or its subject, and add no details the question does not ask for ("Blue", not "Anna's favorite color is blue"; "3 March, 2022", not "Tom moved on 3 March, 2022").
- If the question asks for several things, or for everything of a kind across the conversation, collect all of them from every passage and give them as a comma-separated list of the specific items (not a summary of them), and nothing else; for counts, count every distinct instance.
- If the question asks when something happened or how long it took, resolve relative expressions ("yesterday", "last week", "next month") against the date of the session in which they were said, and give an absolute date, or a duration if the question asks how long. When the conversation only gives a span such as "last week", "last weekend" or "a few days ago", answer relative to that session's date ("the week before 10 March, 2021"), never with only the month or "Week of ...".
- If you mention a person, use their name, never "I".
- For yes/no questions, answer "Yes" or "No".
- Write dates as "D Month, YYYY" (or "Month, YYYY" / "YYYY" when that is all that is known).
If the question contains would, might, could, likely, unlikely, probably, possibly or suspected, it asks for your judgment, so never reply "Not mentioned in the conversation": give the most likely inference from what the conversation shows about that person and common sense, as a short phrase (for example "Likely yes" or "Likely no"). The same holds for a yes/no question about whether the person felt or wanted something that nobody states outright.
Before answering any other question, check whose fact it is and that it is the same thing: the question names a person, and the context must say that this person did, said, owns or experienced the thing asked about. Questions are sometimes built from something the OTHER speaker did or said, with the name swapped, or from the person's own fact with one detail changed: another kind of object, activity or event, another place, occasion, purpose or recipient, the opposite action or quality, or a bigger role (organized where they only took part). Reply exactly "Not mentioned in the conversation" if the context attributes the fact only to someone else (even when the named person asked about it or reacted to it, and even when the question calls it theirs), if the person's closest fact contradicts a detail the question states, if the context shows they never did it, or if nothing in the context concerns that person and that thing. Never write "None" or a sentence saying the person did not do it, and never give the closest other fact instead. If an extracted fact and the dialogue disagree about who did something, follow the dialogue. Other words for the same thing, a broader or narrower name, and a detail the context does not record are not contradictions.
Otherwise always answer with the most likely option the context supports, even when the information is incomplete. These are not reasons to decline: a date in the question that matches no event of that person, such as the right month in another year (answer about the event that matches the person, the action and the thing, unless the date fits the other speaker's event); a plan or past habit of an activity when the question assumes the person did it (answer with that activity); an order of events the context does not settle (use the session dates); a step that general knowledge supplies (the state or country a named place is in, the creator of a named work), given as one answer.
